04 — The Screening Blindspot

Men are 1.75× less likely to ever be screened

55.9% of men in Cameroon have never had their blood pressure measured. For women, the figure is 32% — already damning, but men are far more invisible to the system. This single gap explains much of the unawareness crisis.

Targeted outreach works. Barbershops, taxi ranks, construction sites, and informal markets are where Cameroonian men spend time. Community-based BP screening at these sites has shown 3× higher uptake than clinic-only approaches in comparable sub-Saharan settings. Mbeh et al., Pan African Medical Journal 2019

07 — Policy Imperatives

A roadmap for national impact

Moving beyond awareness, concrete policy changes are essential to transform the health landscape in Cameroon. These actionable steps can save thousands of lives annually.

Expand Community Screening

Integrate blood pressure screening into existing community health programs and non-traditional settings like markets, barbershops, and workplaces. Equip community health workers with digital tools for data collection and follow-up.

Implement Digital Health Solutions

Develop and deploy a national mobile health platform for patient registration, automated SMS reminders for follow-up appointments, and real-time alerts for healthcare providers on high-risk patients.

Enhance Data Surveillance

Establish a robust national hypertension registry to track prevalence, awareness, treatment, and control rates. Utilize this data to inform targeted interventions and resource allocation, ensuring accountability and continuous improvement.

Foster Public-Private Partnerships

Collaborate with private sector healthcare providers, NGOs, and technology companies to leverage resources, expertise, and innovative solutions for hypertension prevention and management across the country.
