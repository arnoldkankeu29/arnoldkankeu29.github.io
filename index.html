05 — The Screening Blindspot

Men are 1.75× less likely to ever be screened

55.9% of men in Cameroon have never had their blood pressure measured. For women, the figure is 32% — already a crisis, but men are far more invisible to the system. This gender gap explains much of the unawareness crisis.

Non-clinical settings achieve 3× higher uptake. Barbershops, taxi ranks, construction sites, and markets are where Cameroonian men spend time. Community-based BP screening at these locations consistently outperforms clinic-only approaches across comparable sub-Saharan settings.

08 — Policy Imperatives

A roadmap for national impact

Moving beyond awareness, these concrete structural changes could save thousands of lives annually.

Expand Community Screening

Integrate BP screening into barbershops, taxi ranks, markets, and construction sites — where Cameroonian men actually are. Equip community health workers with digital tools for real-time data capture and patient follow-up.

Build Digital Follow-up Infrastructure

Deploy a national mobile health platform for patient registration, automated SMS reminders, and real-time provider alerts for high-risk patients who go silent after an elevated reading.

Create a National HTN Registry

Establish systematic tracking of prevalence, awareness, treatment, and control rates at the regional level. Data must drive resource allocation — not assumptions. The registry is the early-warning system.

Mandate Public-Private Data Sharing

Require private providers to submit de-identified hypertension data to a national registry. Incentivize pharmaceutical companies to fund community-level distribution of low-cost antihypertensives in rural zones.

The remedy
already exists.

Community health workers. A BP logging app. Automated SMS follow-up for flagged patients. Nurse alerts before a silent crisis becomes a stroke. The technology is not the barrier. The political will and infrastructure are.
